# The Potter Sanctuary — business card

Print-ready business card artwork. **Standalone design asset — no application
code, routes, components or data are involved.**

Two options, both sharing the same front:

| Option | Front | Reverse |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Sage, logo left, name, email, QR | Clean sage-on-cream inverse — logo + name + web address |
| **B** | *(identical)* | Sage-on-cream inverse — logo + the four treatments |

## Files

```
out/option-a.pdf        Option A, 2 pages (front, back) — RGB master  ← give this to the printer
out/option-b.pdf        Option B, 2 pages (front, back) — RGB master
out/option-a-CMYK.pdf   Option A converted to DeviceCMYK (see caveat below)
out/option-b-CMYK.pdf   Option B converted to DeviceCMYK
out/proof-guides.pdf    All three faces with trim + safe-area guides (NOT for print)
out/*-p1.png,-p2.png    300 DPI raster proofs
out/*.html              Self-contained HTML (fonts/logo/QR inlined) — what the PDFs were rendered from

src/card.css            Editable stylesheet — all geometry and type
src/front.html          Front face markup
src/back-a.html         Reverse, option A
src/back-b.html         Reverse, option B
fonts/                  Cormorant Garamond + Lora (latin subset, from the site build)
assets/                 logo-cream.png (as shipped), logo-sage.png (recoloured), logo-sage-deep.png
qr-thepottersanctuary.svg   The QR, vector

build.py                Regenerate everything (HTML → PDF → CMYK → proofs)
verify.py               Re-check geometry, QR decode, scan robustness, CMYK separation
```

Regenerate after editing `src/`:

```bash
python3 design/business-card/build.py && python3 design/business-card/verify.py
```

Requires `segno`, `playwright`, `pillow`, `opencv-python-headless`, `pikepdf` and
Ghostscript. Chromium is used headless for rendering.

## Specification

| | |
|---|---|
| Trim size | **85 × 55 mm** (UK/ISO standard) |
| Bleed | **3 mm** all round |
| Artboard / PDF page | **91 × 61 mm** (verified: 91.02 × 61.04 mm, a 0.02 mm rounding artefact of the pt conversion — far inside any press tolerance) |
| Safe margin | **4 mm** inside trim; no text or logo comes closer. Verified visually in `proof-guides.pdf` |
| Resolution | Text and QR are **vector**; the only raster is the logo, placed at ~493 DPI |
| Colour | RGB master + a DeviceCMYK conversion (caveat below) |

## Logo — which file, and is it good enough for print?

**Used: `public/sanctuary-logo.png`** — the same asset the branded emails use.

- **Pixel size: 272 × 382.** The visible artwork occupies 208 × 333 px of that
  canvas; the rest is transparent padding.
- **Placed at 14 mm wide on the front, 15 mm on the reverse** → **≈493 DPI and
  ≈460 DPI** respectively. Both are comfortably above the 300 DPI print standard.
- **Ceiling: ~23 mm wide before it drops below 300 DPI.** Do not scale it larger
  than that without a better source.

**There is no SVG version of the logo anywhere in the repo** — `public/` contains
only PNGs (`sanctuary-logo.png`, `icon-192/512.png`, `apple-touch-icon.png`,
`favicon.ico`). At the size used here that is genuinely fine, but a vector logo
would be better long-term: it would allow any size (signage, banners, large
print) and would separate more cleanly. **Worth commissioning an SVG/AI version
of the flower mark.**

The reverse needs the logo in sage, and the shipped asset is cream — invisible on
a cream background. `assets/logo-sage.png` is generated by `build.py` from the
original: the artwork and its alpha channel are untouched, only the ink colour is
replaced. No redrawing, no quality loss.

## QR code

- **Encodes exactly `https://thepottersanctuary.co.uk`** (no trailing slash, no
  tracking parameters).
- **Vector** — generated as SVG and preserved as vector paths in the PDF (the
  only raster in the file is the logo).
- **Version 3, 29 × 29 modules, error correction level Q** (25% recovery, so it
  still reads with ink spread, a scuff or a thumb over a corner).
- **Symbol 13.0 mm** on an 18 mm cream panel → **≈0.45 mm modules**, above the
  ~0.4 mm practical minimum for phone cameras.
- **Quiet zone**: the spec-required 4 modules are built into the SVG, and the
  cream panel adds more around it.

**Does it scan? Tested, not assumed.** Decoded with OpenCV from the actual
rendered 300 DPI artwork — exact string match. Then re-tested against
progressively degraded copies: it still decodes from a **600 px-wide capture of
the whole card, both sharp and blurred**. A phone photographing a business card
typically captures well over 1000 px across, so there is a wide margin. It also
decodes from the CMYK file after separation.

## Colour

| Element | sRGB | Measured CMYK in the delivered file | Ink coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sage (front background) | `#8A9E85` | C48 M27 Y52 K2 | 129% |
| Cream (reverse background, QR panel) | `#F5F0E8` | C2 M4 Y7 K0 | 13% |
| Bone (front type) | `#FBF7EC` | C0 M2 Y6 K0 | 8% |
| Sage-deep (reverse type) | `#6E8068` | — | — |
| QR modules | `#1C1C1C` | **C0 M0 Y0 K100** | 100% |

Colours are the canonical brand tokens, cross-checked against `app/globals.css`,
`app/admin/admin.css` and `emails/_shared.tsx`.

**One brand inconsistency worth knowing:** `app/globals.css` defines `--cream` as
`#EFE8D6`, while `admin.css` and the email templates both use `#F5F0E8`. The card
uses **`#F5F0E8`** (two sources out of three, and the value specified for this
job). Worth reconciling in the app at some point.

**The QR is forced to 100% K.** Ghostscript's default separation turned it into a
four-colour black (C72 M68 Y67 K88, 280% coverage) — on 0.45 mm modules any plate
misregistration would soften the edges and could break scanning, and 280% exceeds
many presses' ink limits. `build.py` rewrites it to K-only and `verify.py` asserts
it. Maximum coverage anywhere on the card is now **129%**.

## Fonts

The site's own typefaces, taken from the built font cache and embedded:

- **Cormorant Garamond** — business name, treatment names
- **Lora** — email address, tagline, small caps

Both are Google Fonts under the **SIL Open Font License**, which permits
embedding and redistribution.

Chromium emits these as **Type 3 fonts containing vector outlines** (it falls
back to Type 3 for variable fonts). Verified by inspecting the glyph procedures:
they are path operators, **not bitmaps**, so type prints sharp at any size.
Side effect: text is not selectable or searchable in the PDF. For print this is
harmless — outlined type is what many printers prefer anyway, since it removes
any risk of font substitution.

## What a printer will need that is not in this folder

1. **An ICC/press profile.** The CMYK conversion is **unmanaged** — Ghostscript's
   generic profile, not FOGRA39, GRACoL or your printer's own. **Recommended:
   send `option-a.pdf` / `option-b.pdf` (the RGB masters) and let the printer
   separate with their profile.** The `-CMYK.pdf` files are there for printers who
   require CMYK on delivery; if they use them, ask them to keep the QR at 100% K.
2. **A printed proof.** Sage is a muted mid-tone and will shift depending on
   stock and coating. Approve a physical proof before a full run.
3. **Crop marks / registration marks.** Not included — the page is exactly trim +
   bleed, which is what most online printers want (they impose and add marks).
   If yours requires marks in the file, they need a larger media box; say so and
   it can be regenerated.
4. **A vector logo.** See above — PNG only at present.
5. **Stock, weight and finish.** Not specified here. A soft-touch or uncoated
   stock suits the brand; note that uncoated stock has a lower ink limit and will
   darken the sage slightly.
6. **A Pantone match**, if you ever want exact sage consistency across print
   runs and suppliers. Deliberately not guessed at here — it should be chosen
   against a physical Pantone book under controlled lighting.
